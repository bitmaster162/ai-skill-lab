02AI roleFinds candidate evidence, compares passages, drafts an answer and surfaces uncertainty.

03Human checkpointChecks the primary source, data access and approves the answer before operational use.

04Success signalA pre-agreed reviewer-acceptance and response-time threshold for source-backed answers.

05Stop conditionSources do not cover the question, access rights are unclear or review takes as long as the current process.

GOOD CANDIDATE

Where a pilot makes sense

✓ repeatable knowledge work with a clear input and output
✓ documents, research, classification, drafting or decision preparation with human review
✓ an accountable owner and a describable definition of good output
✓ a bounded pilot without autonomous access to critical actions
STOP SIGNALS

Where another analysis comes first

× no process owner or nobody willing to review the output
× a fully autonomous safety-critical decision is required
× required data cannot be used or privacy boundaries are unclear
× the expectation is one-button replacement without redesign or controls
HANDOFF

Artifacts, not AI theatre.

Current-state map · candidate backlog · prototype / automation · test cases · human checkpoints · privacy/data boundaries · owner & fallback · operating notes.

DECISION GATE

Ship · Revise · Stop.

Before building, define acceptable output: output quality, required human review, acceptable failure modes, data boundaries, fallback and process owner. After testing, the decision follows those criteria rather than how impressive the demo looks.

01

SHIP

Verification criteria pass, the owner accepts the workflow and responsibility for ongoing review is clear.

02

REVISE

There is value, but bounded quality, data, UX or operating-rule issues require another iteration.

03

STOP

Risk, missing ownership, weak quality or data constraints make adoption worse than the current process. There is no obligation to “implement AI”.

FIRST MESSAGE

Six points are enough.

1. Which process you want to improve
2. Who performs it and who owns it
3. How often it repeats
4. Which inputs it uses
5. What a good output looks like
6. What happens if AI is wrong
